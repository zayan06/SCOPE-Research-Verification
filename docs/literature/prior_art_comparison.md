# Prior-art comparison

This is a technical research note for student review, not a claim that SCOPE is novel or effective. “SCOPE proposed difference” refers to the current design and contract; the implementation and evaluation have not established that it works.

## Five closest studies

| Study | Problem and retrieval | Verification and structured scientific information | Scope, qualification, and mismatch behavior | Documented limitation / boundary | SCOPE comparison and remaining test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SciFact** (R02) | Retrieves candidate abstracts for scientific claims. The VERISCI pipeline used TF-IDF retrieval, then a BERT rationale selector and an abstract-level label predictor. | Predicts support, refute, or no information for claim–abstract pairs and identifies rationale sentences. It handles scientific terminology and requires reasoning about numbers/direction, but does not expose a typed field-by-field scope record. | Evidence is abstract-level and labels are claim–abstract stance. A claim may be contradicted by an abstract; there is no separate general `scope_mismatch` outcome in the benchmark taxonomy. SciFact-Open later documents special-case support. | The authors chose abstracts because evidence often appears there and full-text rationale annotation was less reliable; this constrains coverage of methods, tables, and context. See task and annotation design in the [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/). | SCOPE proposes retaining relation labels and evidence traceability while displaying explicit scope comparisons. Test on identical retrieved candidates against SciFact-style text-only classification; do not claim retrieval/rationale/verification as new. |
| **SciFact-Open** (R03) | Tests open-corpus verification over 500K abstracts. Evidence annotation pools the top predictions of four existing verifiers; one retrieval route uses BM25 followed by a neural reranker trained on MS MARCO, alongside the candidate verifiers’ systems. | Annotates evidence and verdicts in an open-corpus setting; shows smaller-corpus systems lose at least 15 F1 and identifies special-case evidence in practice. | It directly surfaces the broad-claim/narrow-evidence phenomenon. It does not provide a comprehensive typed schema for population, dataset, methods, metrics, conditions, numerical agreement, and certainty; absence here means “not the benchmark’s target,” not “the system cannot reason about it.” | Exhaustive evidence labeling over 500K abstracts is infeasible; pooled labels are not exhaustive. See [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.347/) and [dataset documentation](https://github.com/dwadden/scifact-open/blob/main/doc/data.md). | This is the most direct benchmark precedent for overgeneralization. SCOPE’s testable distinction is an explicit, inspectable dimension comparison and a separate mismatch/qualification/abstention policy. Evaluate with pooled-evidence uncertainty visible. |
| **MultiVerS** (R04) | Uses claim plus full-document context, with long-context encoding; its evaluation includes SciFact, COVIDFact, and HealthVer. It is primarily a verifier, with document candidates supplied by the benchmark or retrieval setup. | Jointly predicts a document label and rationale sentences, leveraging weak document labels where sentence rationales are absent; reports gains over two baselines across three datasets, including zero/few-shot transfer. | It does use full context, and the authors explicitly discuss that extracted rationale sentences can lose qualifiers or coreference. Its output remains document-level support/refute/insufficient-style verification with rationales; the published task does not evaluate a general SCOPE dimension vector or distinct scope-mismatch label. | Rationale sentences may omit context needed for interpretation, including qualifiers and coreference; see the paper’s rationale discussion in the [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-naacl.6/). | SCOPE should be compared as a transparent reviewer layer on top of strong context-aware verification. Any benefit must exceed MultiVerS-like evidence/rationale capability and be measured, not inferred from the schema alone. |
| **ARSJoint** (R06) | Jointly learns abstract retrieval, rationale selection, and stance prediction. Candidate abstracts are generated using BioSentVec representations and cosine similarity top-*k*. | A reading-comprehension formulation jointly predicts abstract relevance, rationale, and stance; sentence-level attention regularization links retrieval and rationale selection. Evaluated on SciFact. | Handles claim/evidence stance jointly and preserves an evidence rationale. It does not publish a broad, typed population/dataset/method/metric/condition comparison layer or a separate mismatch taxonomy. This is a benchmark/output boundary, not proof it cannot learn such signals. | The study’s task/data/evaluation are SciFact and abstract-centered, so broader full-text cross-domain scope behavior is not established. This boundary follows the evaluation setup; it is not presented as an explicit author-declared failure. See [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.290/). | ARSJoint is a close architectural comparator. Use the same candidate pool where possible and isolate whether scope features improve relation decisions; retrieval and verifier architecture differences otherwise confound the comparison. |
| **Evidence Inference 2.0** (R07) | The article is supplied by the task; there is no literature-corpus retrieval stage. The model identifies evidence spans in a clinical-trial report and predicts the comparison answer for an intervention, comparator, and outcome prompt. | Explicit ICO fields and directional labels (increase, decrease, no significant difference) make this a strong precedent for structured outcome-conditioned reasoning and evidence localization. | Population is not a separate prompt field: task design assumes it is implicit in the article/question. It handles outcome and treatment comparisons, not a general distinction among support, contradiction, qualification, scope mismatch, and unrelated evidence. | It is restricted to clinical-trial reports and ICO prompts; the paper says participants are omitted because the work focuses on document-level directionality and assumes a single population context. The full-text study reports many rationales outside abstracts and some spanning multiple sentences, motivating context preservation. See [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.bionlp-1.13/). | SCOPE can reuse the idea that a conclusion is conditioned on comparison fields, while broadening the candidate schema beyond intervention/outcome. Evaluate it as a complementary dataset only after label mapping; do not merge labels directly. |

## Coverage matrix: similarities and boundaries

| Capability | Established in nearby work | What SCOPE currently proposes | Evidence required to claim a difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scientific retrieval | TF-IDF/BM25, embedding retrieval, reranking, and joint retrieval are established (R02, R03, R06). | Local lexical baseline and replaceable retrieval adapters. | Recall@k on fixed corpus and matched candidate pools. |
| Evidence verification | SciFact, MultiVerS, and ARSJoint retrieve or consume scientific evidence, predict relations, and expose rationales (R02, R04, R06). | Claim–passage relation output with exact provenance and per-dimension notes. | Matched comparison with strong verifier baselines; rationale adequacy review. |
| Structured study fields | Evidence Inference explicitly prompts intervention/comparator/outcome (R07); SciFact claim/evidence reasoning also involves quantitative/directional information. | Broader fields including population, dataset, methodology, outcome/metric, numeric result, direction, conditions, certainty, and generalization. | Field-level annotation agreement and ablations with human-corrected versus automatic scope. |
| Population/dataset difference | SciFact-Open exposes special-case evidence; Evidence Inference has article-conditioned clinical questions (R03, R07). | `scope_mismatch` is a distinct proposed relation when a relevant known scope difference prevents direct comparison. | Annotated examples and error analysis must show the distinction is stable and useful. |
| Qualification | SciFact-Open reports evidence that supports only a special case; MultiVerS handles full context and notes rationale loss can drop qualifiers (R03, R04). | `qualify` captures narrower or conditional support for a broader assertion. | Annotation precedence and a test set that distinguishes qualification from mismatch and insufficient evidence. |
| Contradiction | Existing verifiers predict refutation/contradiction labels (R02, R04, R06). | Contradiction requires comparable scope and opposing proposition direction. | Measure false contradiction on controlled scope-shift examples and compare to baseline. |
| Abstention/insufficiency | FEVER, SciFact, and PubMedQA contain insufficient/unknown-style outcomes (R01, R02, R08). | Report selective prediction and whether missing evidence/scope triggers abstention. | Risk–coverage analysis and calibrated threshold on held-out data. |
| Researcher workflow | Existing benchmarks emphasize model outputs; rationale-bearing systems provide inspectable evidence (R02, R04). | Local-first workspace and researcher review are product/design goals. | Usability study or reviewer task study; no usability benefit is established yet. |

## Candidate contribution, stated conservatively

A defensible candidate contribution is an evaluated, reviewer-facing representation of scope comparisons for claim–passage alignment, with explicit unknown values and a relation policy that separates support, contradiction, qualification, scope mismatch, insufficient evidence, and unrelated material. Retrieval, scientific claim verification, rationale extraction, outcome-conditioned comparison, and full-document context all have prior art. The potential contribution lies only in the operationalization, annotation, and demonstrated value of the combined scope layer, if a fair comparison supports it.

Before a novelty statement, students should read the full papers, inspect current systems/code, search later work, finalize label precedence, and compare with the strongest feasible baseline. If implementation or evaluation does not support a distinct gain, describe SCOPE as a prototype integration and report the negative or inconclusive result.
